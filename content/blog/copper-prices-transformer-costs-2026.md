**Copper climbed to record highs in 2026, trading above US$14,000 a tonne on the London Metal Exchange (LME) and reaching about US$14,500 at its January peak.** Copper is one of the biggest material costs in a transformer, in the windings, and in switchgear, in the bus and connections. So higher copper prices flow through to equipment prices, usually with a lag of a few months. Together with tight supply of transformer-grade electrical steel, tariffs and strong demand, this is why many quotes now carry short price validity and metal escalation clauses.

*Prices are current to early November 2026. Commodity prices move daily, so check a live source before relying on any figure. Sources are listed at the end.*

## What happened to copper

- **January 2026:** LME copper hit an intraday record of about **US$14,527 a tonne**.
- **During 2026:** prices stayed historically high, at times above **US$14,000 a tonne**. The New York (COMEX) contract set its own records, helped by US tariffs on copper imports.
- **Drivers:** tight mine supply, demand from grids, data centres, EVs and renewables, and trade disruption, including US Section 232 tariffs on copper and copper products.

## How copper and other materials feed into equipment prices

| Material | Where it is used | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | Transformer windings, switchgear bus, cables, connections | Large share of material cost; prices are volatile |
| Grain-oriented electrical steel (GOES) | Transformer cores | Made by only a few producers worldwide; supply has been tight |
| Aluminum | Alternative winding material; bus; enclosures | Cheaper and lighter than copper; also subject to tariffs |
| Carbon steel | Tanks, enclosures, structures | Affected by steel tariffs |
| Insulating fluid and insulation | Liquid-filled transformers; pressboard insulation | Specialty supply chains; new Canadian pressboard capacity is coming |

For many distribution and power transformers, materials make up the largest share of the price, so swings in copper and steel show up quickly in quotes. Factory capacity is also tight, as our update on [the transformer shortage](/blog/transformer-shortage-canada-2026) explains, so suppliers have little reason to absorb cost increases.

## How suppliers now quote

Expect to see some or all of these on 2026 quotes:

- **Shorter price validity** than buyers were used to a few years ago
- **Metal escalation clauses**, which adjust the price if copper (and sometimes steel) moves more than a set amount between order and shipment, indexed to a published price such as LME or COMEX
- **Tariff adjustment clauses**, covering changes in duties between order and delivery. See [US tariffs and electrical equipment](/blog/us-tariffs-electrical-equipment-canada-2026).
- **Deposits or progress payments** to lock in materials and factory slots

These clauses are not automatically unfair. A fixed price in a volatile market usually means the supplier has priced in a cushion. What matters is that the terms are clear, capped and comparable between bidders.

## Five ways to manage material cost risk

1. **Read the escalation clause closely.** What index is used, what base price and date, what threshold triggers an adjustment, whether it works in both directions, and whether there is a cap.
2. **Compare bids on the same basis.** Ask every bidder for price validity and escalation terms, and note them side by side. Our [RFQ and bid scorecard](/resources/rfq-scorecard) has fields for both.
3. **Consider aluminum windings.** For many distribution and dry-type transformers, aluminum windings perform reliably at lower and more stable cost. They are larger for the same rating, and connections need proper design and torque. Copper still makes sense where space is tight or the specification requires it.
4. **Look at lifetime cost, not just price.** A more efficient transformer can cost more up front and pay back through lower losses. Our [savings and payback calculator](/resources/savings-payback-calculator) compares two units over their life.
5. **Check stock first for urgent needs.** [In-stock equipment](/inventory) was built at earlier material prices and ships quickly.

## Copper vs aluminum windings at a glance

| | Copper | Aluminum |
|---|---|---|
| Conductivity | Higher, so smaller windings for the same rating | Lower, so larger windings for the same rating |
| Size and weight | Smaller, heavier per volume | Larger, but lighter overall |
| Material cost | Higher and more volatile | Lower and generally more stable |
| Connections | Very forgiving | Need proper design, hardware and torque |
| Typical use | Space-constrained installs, some specifications | Most distribution and dry-type transformers |

Both are proven materials. Efficiency standards, such as Canada's CSA C802 minimum efficiency levels, apply regardless of winding material. See our guide to [transformer efficiency rules in Canada](/blog/transformer-efficiency-regulations-canada).

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are transformers so expensive in 2026?

A mix of high copper and electrical steel prices, tariffs, and demand that is outpacing factory capacity. Materials are a large share of a transformer's price, so record copper prices feed through directly.

### How much did copper cost in 2026?

LME copper set an intraday record of about US$14,527 a tonne in January 2026 and traded above US$14,000 for much of the year. Check a live source for today's price.

### What is a metal escalation clause?

A contract term that adjusts the equipment price if the price of copper or another metal moves beyond a set threshold between order and shipment, using a published index such as LME or COMEX.

### Are aluminum-wound transformers as good as copper?

Yes, when designed and connected properly. They are larger for the same rating but usually cost less, and they must meet the same efficiency standards.
