A **load bank** is a portable or permanently installed device that turns electrical power into heat in a controlled way. Connected to a generator, it lets you load the set to a known level — 25%, 50%, 75%, 100% of its rating — and watch how it performs, without depending on the building's own load.

It is the only way to be sure a standby generator can actually carry its rated load before the day it has to.

## Why generators need load bank tests

Most standby generators are tested monthly on whatever load the building happens to provide, which is often far below their rating. That causes three problems:

1. **Wet-stacking.** A lightly loaded diesel never reaches full temperature. Unburnt fuel and carbon collect in the exhaust and injectors, reducing power and reliability. A run at real load burns them off.
2. **Unproven capacity.** A set that has only ever carried 20% of its rating has never shown it can carry 100%. Problems with fuel delivery, cooling, governors and voltage regulation often only appear at high load.
3. **Code compliance.** In Canada, CSA C282 calls for regular testing of emergency generators under meaningful load. Where the building load is too small, a load bank provides it. See [CSA C282 generator testing explained](/blog/csa-c282-generator-testing-requirements).

## Resistive vs resistive-reactive load banks

| | Resistive | Resistive-reactive |
|---|---|---|
| Load type | Pure kW (unity power factor) | kW plus kVAR, typically at 0.8 power factor |
| Tests | Engine, cooling, fuel, governor | Engine plus alternator, voltage regulator and paralleling |
| Typical use | Routine annual and maintenance tests | Commissioning, paralleled sets, critical facilities |
| Size and cost | Smaller and lower cost | Larger and more expensive |

Generators are rated in kVA at 0.8 power factor. A **resistive** test loads the engine fully but never draws the alternator's full rated current. A **resistive-reactive** test does, which is why commissioning of hospitals, data centres and paralleled plants usually calls for one.

## Sizing the load bank

- Match the load bank's **kW rating** to at least the generator's kW rating (for full-load tests).
- Match the **voltage** (208, 480 or 600 V in Canada) and frequency.
- Check **load steps**: fine steps let you hold an exact percentage.
- For paralleled systems, size for the combined rating or test sets individually.
- Plan the **heat**: a 1,000 kW load bank discharges 1 MW of heat, so placement and airflow matter, especially indoors or in parking structures.

## A typical step-load test

Procedures vary with the specification, but a common pattern is:

1. **Pre-checks:** fluids, batteries, connections, safety barriers and cable sizing
2. **Warm-up:** run unloaded or lightly loaded until temperatures stabilize
3. **Step load:** increase in steps — for example 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% — holding each step long enough for readings to stabilize
4. **Record at each step:** kW, voltage, frequency, current per phase, oil pressure, coolant and exhaust temperatures
5. **Transient test (if specified):** apply and remove large blocks of load to check voltage and frequency recovery
6. **Cool-down:** reduce load gradually and let the engine cool before shutdown
7. **Report:** readings, any defects and pass/fail against the specification

## Portable, trailer or permanent?

- **Portable** load banks (typically up to a few hundred kW) suit periodic testing of smaller sets.
- **Trailer-mounted** units serve service fleets testing larger generators.
- **Permanent outdoor** load banks are wired to the generator and can run automatic exercise tests, topping up the building load during monthly runs — a good answer when a building can never reach 30% on its own.
- **Containerized and medium-voltage** load banks test multi-megawatt plants.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a generator be load bank tested?

Many owners test annually, and more often where monthly runs cannot reach a meaningful load. Follow CSA C282, CSA Z32 for health care, and your authority having jurisdiction.

### How long does a load bank test take?

A typical maintenance test runs from one to a few hours including warm-up, the load steps and cool-down. Commissioning tests are often longer.

### Can a load bank test damage a generator?

A properly run test within the generator's rating is what the set is designed for. It often reveals existing weaknesses — which is exactly the point — so problems found during a test should be fixed before the set is relied on.

### What is wet-stacking?

The build-up of unburnt fuel and carbon in a diesel engine's exhaust system from running at light load. It shows up as black, oily residue at exhaust joints and reduced performance. Running at real load burns it off.
