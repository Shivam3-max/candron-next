**A factory visit is the one chance to see inside a transformer before it is sealed for 30 years.** Test reports tell you the unit passed. A walkthrough of the core and coil, the tank and the finish tells you how carefully it was built. This guide follows a transformer through the factory in the order an inspector sees it, and lists what good looks like at each stop.

## Plan the visit

- Agree the **inspection and test plan (ITP)** in the purchase order: which tests are witnessed, hold points and acceptance criteria.
- Book the visit early. Witness dates are usually confirmed two to four weeks ahead.
- Bring the approved drawings, specification and a copy of guaranteed losses and impedance.
- If possible, schedule a **core-and-coil inspection** before tanking, not just the final test.

## Stop 1: Coil assembly

What to check:

- **Winding tension and tightness**: turns tightly wound with no bulges or loose layers.
- **Spacers and ducts**: evenly placed and aligned so fluid or air flows freely.
- **Clamping and bracing**: windings firmly clamped to withstand short-circuit forces.
- **Clean conductors and insulation**: no damaged paper, debris or contamination.
- **Lead exits**: insulated, supported and clear of sharp edges.

## Stop 2: Core construction

- **Lamination stacking**: neat, with tight joints and no visible gaps or burrs.
- **Step-lap joints** where specified. See [core construction](/blog/transformer-core-construction).
- **Core clamping**: bolts and bands tight, clamps insulated where required.
- **Single core ground**: confirm one ground connection, and ask to see the core-ground insulation test.

## Stop 3: Leads, insulation and internal wiring

- Leads supported at regular intervals and clamped with insulating cleats.
- Adequate clearances between leads and to the tank.
- Tap changer leads neatly routed and correctly identified.
- Brazed or crimped joints clean and insulated.

## Stop 4: Bolt torque

Bolted connections inside and outside the tank should be torqued to specification and **marked with torque-seal paint** so you can see they were checked. Spot-check a few with the factory's calibrated torque wrench.

## Stop 5: Drying and tanking

- The active part is dried, by vapour-phase or oven-and-vacuum, to remove moisture from the paper. Ask for the drying record and end-point criteria.
- After tanking, the unit is vacuum-filled with fluid and left to impregnate before testing.

## Stop 6: Tank welds and pressure test

- Welds smooth and continuous, with no porosity, undercut or spatter.
- **Leak test**: the tank held under pressure for several hours with no drop and no visible weeps.
- Gaskets seated evenly in their grooves.

## Stop 7: Paint and exterior finish

- Surface blasted before painting.
- **Dry film thickness** measured with a gauge against the specified system.
- No runs, bare spots or damage around lifting lugs and edges.
- Nameplate correct and securely fixed.

## Stop 8: Radiators and accessories

- Radiators flushed clean, valves operate, fins undamaged.
- Gauges, PRD, fans and control wiring installed and functionally tested.
- Control cabinet wiring neat, labelled and matching the schematic.

## The routine tests to witness

| Test | What it confirms |
|---|---|
| Ratio, polarity and phase relation | Correct turns and connections on every tap |
| Winding resistance | Good joints and connections |
| No-load loss and excitation current | Core quality; compare with the guarantee |
| Load loss and impedance | Winding design; compare with the guarantee |
| Applied and induced potential | Insulation withstand |
| Insulation resistance and power factor | Dryness and insulation condition |
| Leak test | Tank integrity |
| Impulse and temperature rise (if specified) | Surge withstand and thermal design |

Compare measured losses and impedance with the **guaranteed values** before you leave. See [transformer testing types](/blog/transformer-testing-types).

## After the visit

- Get signed test reports and the inspection record.
- Agree corrective actions in writing before release to ship.
- Confirm shipping arrangements: impact recorder, nitrogen pressure, blocking.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I inspect during a transformer factory visit?

The coils, core, leads and internal wiring before tanking, then bolt torque, tank welds, paint, radiators and accessories, followed by the routine tests listed in your inspection and test plan.

### Which transformer tests should be witnessed?

At minimum ratio, winding resistance, no-load and load losses with impedance, applied and induced potential, and insulation tests. Add impulse and temperature rise when they are specified.

### Is a factory acceptance test enough without a core-and-coil inspection?

Tests confirm performance, but they do not show workmanship hidden inside the tank. For critical or large units, a core-and-coil inspection before tanking adds real assurance.

### What happens if a transformer fails a factory test?

The supplier investigates and corrects the cause, then repeats the test. Agree the corrective action and retest plan before the unit ships.
