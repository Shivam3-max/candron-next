**The moment a transformer arrives on site is your best chance to catch shipping damage and record it while the carrier is still responsible.** Once the bill of lading is signed "received in good condition", proving later that damage happened in transit becomes much harder. A 30-minute inspection with a clear checklist protects the project schedule and the warranty.

## Before the truck arrives

- Confirm the **offloading method** (crane, forklift, jacks and rollers) and that equipment is rated for the shipping weight on the drawings.
- Prepare a **level, compacted** offloading and storage area, or the final pad.
- Have the **outline drawing, nameplate data and packing list** to hand.
- Arrange for someone authorized to note exceptions on the bill of lading.

## 1. Check the impact recorder

Most substation and many large distribution transformers ship with an **impact (shock) recorder** that logs acceleration in three directions.

- Check it **before** offloading and photograph the reading.
- Compare against the manufacturer's acceptance limits, typically a few g.
- If limits are exceeded, note it on the bill of lading and contact the supplier before energizing. Internal tests may be required.

## 2. External inspection

Walk around the unit and photograph everything:

- **Tank**: dents, scrapes, weld cracks, paint damage.
- **Radiators and fins**: bent or crushed fins, leaks at flanges.
- **Bushings**: chipped porcelain, cracks, loose clamps. Padmount bushing wells undamaged.
- **Gauges**: glass intact, readings plausible.
- **Fluid leaks**: wet spots under gaskets, valves and drains.
- **Blocking and tie-downs**: still in place and intact, showing the load did not shift.

## 3. Pressure and fluid level

- **Sealed tanks**: the pressure gauge should show a positive or slightly negative reading, not zero over several days. Units shipped with a nitrogen charge should hold their positive pressure.
- **Fluid level**: at the expected mark for the temperature.
- **Units shipped without oil** (large power transformers) are filled with dry gas: confirm the gas pressure and dew point are within the supplier's limits.

## 5. Accessories and loose parts

- Count crates and loose items against the packing list: arresters, radiators, fans, conservator parts, gaskets, hardware, keys.
- Check the **document pack**: test report, outline drawing, nameplate drawing, wiring diagrams and O&M manual.

## 6. Record exceptions

- Write every damage or shortage on the **bill of lading before signing**, with photos.
- Notify the carrier and supplier in writing within the time limit in the terms of sale, often 48 hours or less for concealed damage.

## Storage on site

If the transformer will not be installed immediately:

- keep it on a level, well-drained surface,
- keep it **sealed**; do not open covers or inspection ports,
- check pressure and fluid level regularly,
- for **dry-type units**, store indoors, dry and covered, with space heaters energized if supplied,
- protect bushings and accessories from damage and theft.

## Before energizing

Typical pre-energization checks, coordinated with the commissioning plan:

- insulation resistance of windings and core ground,
- turns ratio on the selected tap,
- winding resistance,
- fluid dielectric test (and DGA baseline) where required,
- correct tap position, grounding and connections torqued,
- gauges, alarms and trips functionally tested.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I check when a transformer is delivered?

The impact recorder, external damage, bushings, gauges, fluid level and pressure, nameplate against the order, loose parts and documents, then record any exceptions on the bill of lading before signing.

### What is an impact recorder on a transformer?

A device that logs shocks during transport. Readings above the manufacturer's limits mean the core and windings may have shifted, and internal checks are needed before energizing.

### Can I store a transformer outdoors before installation?

Liquid-filled transformers can be stored outdoors if kept sealed and level. Dry-type transformers must be stored indoors, dry and protected.

### What tests are done before energizing a new transformer?

Typically insulation resistance, turns ratio, winding resistance, fluid tests where applicable, and functional checks of gauges and protection.
