**A bushing is the insulated passage that carries a conductor safely through the grounded steel tank of a transformer.** It has to hold back full system voltage, carry full load current, survive lightning surges and seal the tank against moisture for decades. Bushings are small parts, but they are among the most common sources of transformer failure, so it is worth knowing what you are specifying.

## What a bushing must do

- **Insulate**: withstand rated voltage and impulse voltage (BIL) between the conductor and the tank.
- **Conduct**: carry rated current without overheating.
- **Seal**: keep fluid in and moisture out.
- **Survive**: handle mechanical loads from cables and bus, weather, pollution and UV.

## Bushing markings

On North American transformers, HV bushings are marked **H0, H1, H2, H3** and LV bushings **X0, X1, X2, X3**. H0 and X0 are neutrals. Markings match the nameplate connection diagram, so crews can phase and connect correctly. Our [nameplate guide](/blog/how-to-read-a-transformer-nameplate) explains the diagram.

## Types of bushing

### Porcelain bushings

The traditional choice: a glazed porcelain body with a central conductor. Strong, UV-proof and proven over a century of service. Downsides: heavy, brittle if struck, and they can shatter in a fault.

### Polymer (silicone) bushings

Silicone rubber housings over a fibreglass or epoxy core. Lighter, shatter-resistant and very good in polluted or coastal areas because the hydrophobic surface sheds water. Common on distribution and medium-voltage substation units.

### Condenser bushings (OIP and RIP)

For higher voltages, typically above about 69 kV, plain insulators are not enough. **Condenser bushings** contain concentric foil layers that grade the electric field evenly.

- **OIP**: oil-impregnated paper.
- **RIP**: resin-impregnated paper, dry and oil-free.

Condenser bushings have a test tap so their **power factor and capacitance** can be measured in the field, one of the best early warnings of bushing deterioration.

### LV bushings and spades

LV bushings carry high current at low voltage. They are often epoxy or porcelain with copper spades or studs drilled for NEMA lug patterns.

## Padmount bushings: wells, inserts and elbows

Padmount transformers are **dead-front**: there are no exposed live parts. The HV side uses:

- **Bushing wells**: the permanent receptacles mounted on the tank.
- **Bushing inserts**: plug into the wells and provide the interface for the cable connector.
- **Elbow connectors**: insulated, shielded cable terminations that plug onto the inserts.

Two families matter:

| Type | Typical rating | Can be operated under load? |
|---|---|---|
| **Loadbreak** | 200 A | Yes, with a hot stick, within its rating |
| **Deadbreak** | 600 A | No, de-energize first |

Parking stands let crews park elbows safely when isolating a unit. See our [padmount transformer guide](/blog/padmount-transformer-guide) for configurations such as loop-feed and radial-feed.

## Ratings to specify

- **Voltage class and BIL**: match the system. See [basic impulse level](/blog/transformer-bil-basic-impulse-level).
- **Current rating**: at least the transformer's full-load current, with margin.
- **Creepage distance**: longer for polluted, coastal or industrial sites.
- **Material**: porcelain or polymer.
- **Connector type**: elbows, spades or studs to match your cables.

## Maintenance and warning signs

- Cracked or chipped porcelain, or tracking marks on polymer.
- Fluid leaks at the gasket.
- Hot connections on an infrared scan.
- Rising power factor or capacitance on condenser bushings.
- Contamination build-up in dirty environments; clean during outages.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a transformer bushing?

An insulated device that carries a conductor through the transformer tank wall, insulating it from the grounded tank and sealing the tank.

### What is the difference between loadbreak and deadbreak elbows?

Loadbreak elbows, typically 200 A, can be operated under load with a hot stick. Deadbreak connectors, typically 600 A, must only be connected or disconnected when de-energized.

### Are polymer bushings better than porcelain?

Polymer bushings are lighter, shatter-resistant and perform well in pollution. Porcelain is proven and UV-proof. Both are reliable when rated correctly.

### What do H1 and X1 mean on a transformer?

H1 is the first high-voltage terminal and X1 the first low-voltage terminal, as shown on the nameplate connection diagram.
