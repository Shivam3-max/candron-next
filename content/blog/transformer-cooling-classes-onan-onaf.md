**A transformer cooling class is a four-letter code that describes what is inside the tank and how heat is moved out.** ONAN means oil, natural circulation, air, natural circulation: no fans and no pumps. ONAF adds fans. Each extra cooling stage raises the load the transformer can carry without exceeding its temperature limits, which is why substation transformers often carry two or three MVA ratings on one nameplate.

## How to read the code

| Letter | Position | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **O** / **K** / **L** | 1st: internal coolant | O = mineral oil (flash point ≤ 300 °C); K = less-flammable fluid such as natural ester or silicone (fire point > 300 °C); L = no measurable fire point |
| **N** / **F** / **D** | 2nd: internal circulation | N = natural (thermosiphon); F = forced through the cooler; D = directed through the windings |
| **A** / **W** | 3rd: external coolant | A = air; W = water |
| **N** / **F** | 4th: external circulation | N = natural convection; F = fans or pumps |

So **ONAN** = oil, natural, air, natural. **KNAN** is the same with a less-flammable fluid such as natural ester. **ODAF** = oil pumped directly through the windings, with fans on the coolers.

## Common classes

- **ONAN**: radiators only. Quiet, simple, very reliable. Standard for distribution and smaller substation units.
- **ONAN/ONAF**: radiators plus fans that start on temperature. The most common substation arrangement.
- **ONAN/ONAF/ONAF**: two fan stages for a third rating.
- **OFAF / ODAF**: pumps and fans, used on large power transformers where compact coolers are needed.
- **OFWF**: oil-to-water heat exchangers, used where air cooling is impractical, such as some hydro plants.

## Multiple ratings on one nameplate

A substation transformer might be rated **12/16/20 MVA ONAN/ONAF/ONAF**:

- 12 MVA with fans off,
- 16 MVA with the first fan group running,
- 20 MVA with all fans running.

Each fan stage typically adds about 25–33% to the base rating. Fans are controlled by top-oil and winding-temperature indicators, so they only run when needed.

**Design tip:** size the base ONAN rating for normal load and use fan stages for emergency or contingency load. Fans then run rarely, saving energy and wear.

## Dry-type cooling classes

Dry-type transformers use simpler codes:

- **AN**: air natural, ventilated enclosure.
- **AF**: air forced, fans added.
- **AN/AF**: dual rating, with fans typically adding about 33% capacity.

Fans on dry-type units suit temporary or peak loads. For continuous loads, it is better to size the AN rating correctly than to rely on fans that need maintenance.

## What affects cooling performance in service

- **Radiator cleanliness**: dust, pollen and debris block airflow.
- **Clearances**: walls and enclosures restrict airflow; keep to the manufacturer's clearances.
- **Fan control and maintenance**: test fan starts during maintenance.
- **Ambient**: cold climates help cooling, but very cold starts need attention for viscous fluids. See our [extreme cold guide](/blog/electrical-equipment-extreme-cold).
- **Fluid condition**: sludge reduces circulation.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between ONAN and ONAF?

ONAN cools with natural oil circulation and natural air convection only. ONAF adds fans blowing over the radiators, which raises the rating of the same transformer.

### How much does ONAF add to a transformer rating?

Typically about 25–33% per fan stage, so a 12 MVA ONAN unit might be rated 16 MVA ONAF and 20 MVA with a second fan stage.

### What does KNAN mean?

The same as ONAN, but with a less-flammable fluid, such as natural ester, with a fire point above 300 °C instead of mineral oil.

### Should I specify fans or a larger transformer?

For loads that are high most of the time, a larger ONAN rating is more reliable and efficient. Fans make sense for occasional peaks and contingency loading.
