**The gauges and protective devices on a transformer are its early-warning system.** They report fluid level, temperature, pressure and gas, and they trip or alarm before a developing fault becomes a fire or a failed unit. Knowing what each device does, and what normal looks like, turns a routine walk-around into real preventive maintenance.

## Liquid level gauge

Shows the fluid level relative to the **25 °C mark**. The level rises and falls with temperature, so compare readings with the temperature at the time.

- **Low level** may mean a leak. Running with low fluid can expose live parts and cause flashover.
- Many gauges have a low-level alarm contact.

## Top-oil temperature indicator

A dial thermometer measuring the hottest fluid at the top of the tank, with a **drag hand** that records the maximum since it was last reset.

- Read the drag hand on every inspection to see peak loading.
- Contacts can start fans and raise alarms.

## Winding temperature indicator (WTI)

The windings are hotter than the oil, but you cannot put a thermometer on them in service. A WTI uses a **thermal image**: a current transformer feeds a small heater around the thermometer bulb, simulating the winding hot spot. Some modern units use **fibre-optic probes** placed directly in the winding during manufacture.

The WTI usually controls fan stages and gives the most accurate overload alarm.

## Pressure-vacuum gauge

On **sealed-tank** transformers, the gas space above the fluid rises and falls in pressure with temperature.

- Normal: slightly positive when hot, slightly negative when cold.
- **Always zero**: the tank may have a leak to atmosphere, letting moisture in.
- **Very high**: may indicate overloading or gas generation.

## Pressure relief device (PRD)

A spring-loaded valve that opens instantly to release a sudden pressure rise, such as an internal arc, protecting the tank from rupture. It reseals after operating and normally has a visible flag and an alarm or trip contact.

If a PRD has operated, **do not re-energize** until the cause is found. Test the fluid for dissolved gases first.

## Sudden pressure relay (SPR)

Detects a **rapid rate of rise** of pressure, which is the signature of an internal fault, while ignoring slow changes from load and temperature. It trips the transformer quickly, limiting damage. Standard on substation transformers. It is often referred to by its device number, 63.

## Buchholz relay

Fitted in the pipe between the main tank and the **conservator** on conservator-type transformers.

- **Slow gas accumulation** (alarm stage): indicates a developing fault such as overheating or partial discharge.
- **Fluid surge** (trip stage): indicates a serious internal fault.

Gas collected in the relay can be sampled and analyzed to identify the fault.

## Dehydrating breather

On conservator units, air entering the conservator passes through silica gel that removes moisture. The gel changes colour as it saturates; replace it before it is fully spent.

## Online DGA and condition monitors

Larger or critical transformers increasingly carry **online dissolved gas analysis** monitors that track hydrogen and other fault gases continuously, along with moisture and load. They catch fast-developing faults between annual oil samples. See [transformer oil testing and DGA](/blog/transformer-oil-testing-dga-explained).

## Quick inspection checklist

| Device | What to record | Act if |
|---|---|---|
| Liquid level | Level and temperature | Below normal for temperature |
| Top-oil thermometer | Reading and drag hand | Drag hand near alarm setting |
| WTI | Reading and drag hand | Repeated high peaks |
| Pressure-vacuum gauge | Pressure | Always zero, or unusually high |
| PRD | Flag status | Flag up: investigate before re-energizing |
| Breather | Silica gel colour | Mostly saturated |
| Fans | Run test | Fan fails to start |

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a pressure relief device do on a transformer?

It opens almost instantly to vent a sudden internal pressure rise, protecting the tank from rupturing during an internal fault, then reseals.

### What is the difference between a Buchholz relay and a sudden pressure relay?

A Buchholz relay is used on conservator transformers and detects both slow gas build-up and fluid surges. A sudden pressure relay detects a rapid pressure rise and is common on sealed and conservator units alike.

### Why does my transformer pressure gauge always read zero?

A gauge that never moves on a sealed tank often means a leak to atmosphere, which lets moisture in. Check gaskets, fittings and the gauge itself.

### How often should transformer gauges be checked?

Monthly visual readings are common for substation units, with full functional testing of alarms and trips during scheduled maintenance.
