**Data centre developers have asked Canadian grids for far more power than the grids can deliver in the next few years, and provinces are now rationing connections.** Alberta has a waiting list of nearly 20,000 MW of new data centre load against an interim limit of 1,200 MW, all of it already allocated. Ontario's grid operator now expects data centres to make up a much larger share of future demand than it forecast a year ago. Quebec requires ministerial approval for large new loads. For the electrical supply chain, the projects that do proceed are among the largest buyers of switchgear, transformers, generators and load banks.

*Figures are current to early November 2026. Sources are listed at the end.*

## Alberta: big queue, small door

| Item | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| AESO interim limit for large loads, through 2028 | 1,200 MW | AESO |
| Status of the 1,200 MW | Fully allocated; contracted projects are proceeding | AESO |
| New data centre load on the connection list | about 19,565 MW (as of 30 July 2026) | Government of Alberta |

The Alberta Electric System Operator (AESO) set the 1,200 MW limit in 2025 as the most additional large load the grid could take without hurting reliability. Qualifying projects shared it pro rata, and it is now fully allocated. Projects without an allocation move to the next phase of the AESO's large-load work, which includes a **bring-your-own-generation** pathway. The province says projects that bring their own new generation will be prioritized.

**What it means:** Alberta data centres that proceed will often include on-site generation. That means generator step-up transformers, medium-voltage switchgear, paralleling gear and protection, alongside the usual data hall equipment.

## Ontario: demand forecast revised up for data centres

The Independent Electricity System Operator's (IESO) **2026 Annual Planning Outlook** forecasts Ontario's annual electricity demand rising from about **152 TWh in 2026 to 250 TWh in 2050** in its reference scenario. That is roughly 65% growth. The total is a little lower than last year's outlook, mainly because of slower EV adoption and more energy efficiency. Data centres moved the other way: the IESO now expects them to reach **8.6% of Ontario demand by 2050**, about 60% more than it previously forecast.

Data centre connection requests in Ontario run to several thousand megawatts. Law firm Torys reported in December 2025 that more than 6,500 MW of data centre projects had requested connection. Many of those projects are early-stage and some will not proceed. The IESO's **South and Central Bulk Plan** (August 2026) lists data centres among the drivers of new load and recommends new transmission stations in several municipalities.

## Quebec: approval first

Quebec requires **ministerial authorization for new loads of 5 MW or more**, a rule introduced through Bill 69. Hydro-Québec has strong demand from industry and electrification as well as data centres, and it is managing which large loads connect and on what terms.

## What it means for electrical equipment

Data centre projects that clear the grid hurdle are equipment-intensive:

- **Medium-voltage switchgear**, often main-tie-main lineups at 13.8–27.6 kV, or a dedicated high-voltage substation for large campuses
- **Unit substation transformers and padmounts** feeding 600 V distribution
- **Generators and paralleling switchgear**, more so where projects must bring their own power
- **UPS, PDUs and remote power panels** in the data halls
- **Load banks** for integrated systems testing before the IT load arrives

Each of those competes for the same factory capacity as utility and renewable projects, which keeps lead times long. ## What contractors and owners can do

1. **Treat grid capacity as the first long-lead item.** Confirm the connection path (allocation, bring-your-own-generation, phased load) before ordering equipment.
2. **Design for phased energization.** Size medium-voltage gear and substations for the full campus, but phase transformers, UPS and PDUs as load is approved.
3. **Order the switchgear and transformers early,** from the single-line diagram, with the [long-lead equipment planner](/resources/lead-time-planner).
4. **Plan commissioning load early.** Integrated systems testing needs a lot of load bank capacity, and rental fleets book up when several halls commission at once.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much power are data centres requesting in Alberta?

About 19,565 MW of new data centre load was on Alberta's connection list as of 30 July 2026, against an interim large-load limit of 1,200 MW that is already fully allocated.

### Can new data centres still connect in Alberta?

Not under the interim 1,200 MW limit, which is allocated. New projects are being considered in the next phase, with priority for projects that bring their own generation.

### How much of Ontario's electricity will data centres use?

The IESO's 2026 outlook expects data centres to reach about 8.6% of Ontario's electricity demand by 2050 in its reference scenario.

### Do data centres in Quebec need government approval?

Yes. New loads of 5 MW or more need ministerial authorization.
