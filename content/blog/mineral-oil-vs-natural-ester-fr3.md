**Natural ester fluids, made from plant oils and sold under names such as FR3, are steadily replacing mineral oil in distribution and substation transformers.** Their fire point is more than twice that of mineral oil, they are readily biodegradable, and they extend the life of the paper insulation. Mineral oil is still cheaper, thinner in very cold weather and the default in many utility fleets. Here is how to choose.

## The key differences

| Property | Mineral oil | Natural ester |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Refined petroleum | Seed oils (soy, canola, rapeseed) |
| Fire point | About 160–170 °C | About 350–360 °C |
| Fluid class | O (flammable) | K (less-flammable, fire point > 300 °C) |
| Biodegradability | Slow | Readily biodegradable |
| Moisture tolerance | Low | Much higher (absorbs water from paper) |
| Effect on paper life | Baseline | Slows paper ageing significantly |
| Viscosity | Lower | Higher, especially when cold |
| Pour point | About −40 °C or lower | About −20 °C |
| Oxidation stability | Good | Needs sealed tanks; thickens if exposed to air |
| Cost | Lower | Higher per litre |

## Fire safety

With a fire point around 360 °C, natural ester is classed as a **less-flammable (K-class) liquid**. Transformers filled with K-class fluid often qualify for **reduced clearances, smaller or no containment** and simpler fire protection under electrical codes and insurer guidelines, especially indoors, near buildings or in vaults. Confirm the specific requirements with the Canadian Electrical Code, your authority having jurisdiction and your insurer.

For data centres, hospitals, urban vaults and rooftop or near-building padmounts, that alone can justify the cost.

## Environmental protection

Natural esters break down quickly in soil and water and are non-toxic. A leak or spill is easier and cheaper to clean up, which matters near waterways, wells, farmland and environmentally sensitive sites.

## Longer insulation life

Paper insulation ages faster when it is wet. Natural ester draws moisture out of the paper and holds it without losing much dielectric strength. Laboratory and field data show paper in ester ageing several times more slowly than paper in mineral oil at the same temperature. In practice this gives:

- longer insulation life at the same load, or
- more overload capacity for the same life.

Some designs use this to run higher temperature rises or to downsize. See [temperature rise and insulation class](/blog/transformer-temperature-rise-insulation-class).

## Cold-climate considerations in Canada

Natural ester is thicker when cold and has a higher pour point than mineral oil. That does **not** stop it working in Canadian winters:

- An energized transformer keeps its own fluid warm.
- Manufacturers design cooling for the fluid's viscosity.
- Cold starts of a de-energized unit after long outages at very low temperatures need checking against the fluid supplier's cold-start guidance.

For units that may sit de-energized in extreme cold, such as standby or seasonal loads in the far north, discuss cold-start limits before ordering. See our [extreme cold guide](/blog/electrical-equipment-extreme-cold).

## Oxidation: why sealed tanks matter

Natural ester oxidizes and thickens if exposed to air for long periods, so it is used in **sealed-tank** or **air-bag conservator** designs, and should not be left in open drums. Field top-ups must use the same fluid.

## Retrofilling existing transformers

Existing mineral-oil transformers can often be **retrofilled** with natural ester to improve fire safety. A small amount of residual mineral oil remains in the paper, which slightly lowers the fire point, so follow the fluid supplier's retrofill procedure and test the result.

## Which should you specify?

**Natural ester is the stronger choice when:**
- the transformer is indoors, in a vault or close to buildings,
- environmental protection is a priority,
- you want extra overload capacity or longer life,
- insurers or owners require less-flammable fluids.

**Mineral oil remains reasonable when:**
- the unit is outdoors with good clearances,
- the lowest first cost is the priority,
- the fleet standard and spare parts are built around mineral oil,
- the unit may face very cold starts after long de-energized periods.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is FR3 transformer fluid?

A natural ester dielectric fluid made from plant oils, with a fire point around 360 °C. It is a less-flammable, biodegradable alternative to mineral oil.

### Is natural ester fluid suitable for Canadian winters?

Yes, for energized transformers. It is thicker when cold, so check cold-start limits for units that may sit de-energized in extreme temperatures.

### Does natural ester extend transformer life?

Yes. It absorbs moisture from the paper insulation and slows paper ageing, extending insulation life at the same operating temperature.

### Can I mix natural ester and mineral oil?

Small residual amounts occur when retrofilling, but topping up one fluid with the other is not recommended. Use the same fluid as the nameplate shows.
