If your building has an emergency generator — for exit lighting, fire pumps, elevators, life-safety systems or a health care facility's essential power — it has to be inspected and tested on a regular schedule. In Canada that schedule comes from **CSA C282, Emergency electrical power supply for buildings**, which building and fire codes reference across the provinces. Health care facilities also follow **CSA Z32**.

This article lays out a typical C282-aligned programme. It is a practical summary, not the standard: intervals, loads and durations can differ between editions and occupancies, so confirm the details with the current edition and your authority having jurisdiction.

## Why the testing matters

A standby generator spends most of its life switched off. When the power fails, it has to start, take load in seconds and run for hours. Regular inspection catches the slow problems — weak batteries, leaking coolant, a failed block heater — and regular running under load proves the engine, alternator and transfer switch actually work together.

## Weekly inspection

A short walk-round by the owner or building operator, with the set at rest:

- fuel level in the day tank and main tank, and the transfer pump
- engine oil and coolant levels
- block heater on and the engine warm
- battery terminals, electrolyte where applicable, and charger output
- no fuel, oil, coolant or exhaust leaks
- control panel in AUTO, no alarms, lamp test
- the room clear, with louvres and air paths open

## Monthly load run

The monthly test is where most programmes succeed or fail. The common benchmark is to run the set **on load for at least 60 minutes at 30% or more of its nameplate rating**, started through the transfer switch so the whole sequence is proven.

Record:

- kW, voltage and frequency
- oil pressure and coolant temperature
- transfer and retransfer times
- any alarms or defects

Also drain the exhaust condensate trap and check ventilation and other auxiliary equipment.

### The 30% problem

Many buildings cannot load their generator to 30% with real building load, especially newer, efficient buildings or sets sized for future growth. Running a diesel engine lightly month after month causes **wet-stacking**: unburnt fuel and carbon build up in the exhaust and injectors, and the engine may not carry full load when it is needed.

The fix is a **load bank** — either a portable unit for periodic tests or a [permanent outdoor load bank](/products/load-banks/ac/stationary) that tops up the load automatically during monthly runs. Our [load bank testing guide](/blog/generator-load-bank-testing) explains how it works.

## Semi-annual and annual service

A qualified contractor should service the set at least twice a year:

- belts, hoses, filters and coolant condition
- battery load test and charger output
- governor and voltage regulator response
- safety shutdowns: low oil pressure, high temperature, overspeed and overcrank

The **annual** visit typically adds an oil and filter change, a fuel sample, transfer switch maintenance, an infrared scan of connections under load, an exhaust system inspection and — where monthly runs could not reach 30% load — a **load bank test**. Confirm the required load-bank duration in the C282 edition your inspector enforces.

## Records

Inspectors and fire officials ask for the logbook. Keep:

- weekly inspection entries
- monthly test readings
- contractor service reports and load bank test reports
- every defect, who fixed it and when

## Health care: CSA Z32

Hospitals and other health care facilities follow CSA Z32 for their essential electrical systems in addition to C282. Test loads, durations and record-keeping are often stricter, and the facility's own essential electrical system plan sets the details.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often do emergency generators need to be tested in Canada?

A typical CSA C282 programme includes weekly inspections, a monthly run on load, semi-annual and annual service by a qualified contractor, and periodic load bank testing. Confirm the exact intervals with the current edition and your authority having jurisdiction.

### What load is required for a monthly generator test?

The common benchmark is at least 30% of the generator's nameplate rating for at least 60 minutes. If the building cannot provide that load, a load bank is used.

### What happens if my generator never reaches 30% load?

Light running causes wet-stacking — unburnt fuel and carbon in the exhaust — which reduces performance and reliability. A load bank test burns it off and proves the set can carry its rated load.

### Who can do the annual generator service?

A qualified generator service contractor. The owner or operator usually handles the weekly inspections and monthly runs, and keeps the logbook.
