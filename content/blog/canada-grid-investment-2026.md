**Canada's big utilities are planning their largest grid investments in decades.** Hydro One has asked to spend about **$37 billion over five years**. Hydro-Québec's **Action Plan 2035** budgets up to **$185 billion** for new generation and grid. BC Hydro is building the **North Coast Transmission Line** and buying new power through competitive calls. The drivers are the same everywhere: electrification, population growth, industrial projects, data centres and replacing ageing equipment. For contractors and equipment suppliers, this is a long, steady wave of substation, transmission and distribution work. It also keeps demand high for transformers and switchgear.

*Figures are current to early November 2026. Some plans are proposals awaiting regulatory approval. Sources are listed at the end.*

## Ontario: Hydro One and the IESO

Hydro One's proposed five-year plan averages about **$7.4 billion a year**. It aims to add transmission capacity to connect and deliver about **6,000 MW** of new electricity, modernize about **1,400 km** of transmission lines and replace or refurbish more than **85,000 distribution poles**. The Ontario Energy Board reviews the plan through a public process before approving the rates that fund it, so the final numbers may change.

The demand behind it comes from the IESO's **2026 Annual Planning Outlook**: about 65% growth in annual demand by 2050 in the reference scenario, driven above all by electric vehicles, plus data centres, industry and population growth. The IESO's regional plans, such as the August 2026 **South and Central Bulk Plan**, recommend new transformer stations in several fast-growing municipalities.

## Quebec: Action Plan 2035

Hydro-Québec's Action Plan 2035 is the largest program in the country. Hydro-Québec has said it will invest up to **$185 billion** by 2035 to add new hydro, wind, solar and storage capacity and to expand and reinforce its transmission and distribution networks. That includes thousands of kilometres of new transmission lines. Investment is ramping up quarter by quarter, and Hydro-Québec reported record investment in early 2026.

## British Columbia: North Coast and new supply

BC Hydro is building the **North Coast Transmission Line** to supply industrial and LNG electrification in the northwest:

- **Phase 1**: Prince George to Fraser Lake, about 164 km
- **Phase 2**: Fraser Lake to Terrace, about 275 km
- **Phase 3**: north of Terrace, about 350 km of new transmission

BC Hydro's service plan puts the preliminary forecast for Phases 1 and 2 at about **$6 billion**, and its 10-year capital plan is being updated for release later in 2026. On supply, BC Hydro awarded 10 electricity purchase agreements in December 2024 (nearly 5,000 GWh a year), then four more wind agreements in 2026 (about 3,500 GWh a year). The province projects electricity demand growing about **20% by 2030 and 50% by 2050**, with peak demand up 44%.

## What it means for the industry

- **More substation and transmission work** for EPCs and electrical contractors, in long, multi-year programs
- **Sustained demand for power and substation transformers, medium-voltage switchgear, breakers and protection**, which keeps factory capacity booked and lead times long
- **Distribution work everywhere**: poles, padmounts and polemount transformers, switching cubicles and metering
- **Renewable interconnections** from calls for power, each needing collector switchgear, inverter-duty transformers and a project substation
- **Competition for skilled labour** and test crews, especially in peak construction seasons

For private projects, that means longer lead times for anything competing with utility orders, such as large padmounts, unit substations and MV switchgear. Plan around them from the start. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much is Hydro One planning to invest?

Hydro One filed a proposed five-year plan of about $37 billion for 2028–2032 with the Ontario Energy Board on 7 October 2026. It is under review and not yet approved.

### What is Hydro-Québec's Action Plan 2035?

Hydro-Québec's plan to invest up to $185 billion by 2035 in new generation, transmission and distribution to meet rising demand from electrification and industry.

### What is the North Coast Transmission Line?

A 500 kV transmission project in northwest British Columbia, built in phases from Prince George towards Terrace and beyond, to supply industrial and LNG electrification.

### Why are Canadian utilities investing so much now?

Electricity demand is forecast to grow strongly through 2050 from electrification, population growth, industry and data centres, while much existing equipment is reaching the end of its life.
