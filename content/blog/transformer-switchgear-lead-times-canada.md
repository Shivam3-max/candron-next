On many Canadian electrical projects, the equipment that sets the energization date is not the building — it is the transformer or the medium-voltage switchgear. Demand from data centres, electrification, renewables and utility grid upgrades has stretched lead times across North America, and the projects that stay on schedule are the ones that treat long-lead equipment as a planning problem from day one.

## Typical lead-time ranges

These are indicative ranges in weeks from **approved drawings** to shipment, from our quarterly [Lead-Time Index](/resources/lead-time-index). Real quotes vary by rating, options and manufacturer.

| Equipment | Typical range (weeks) |
|---|---|
| LV dry-type transformer (≤ 600 V) | 10–26 |
| MV dry-type transformer | 26–52 |
| Three-phase padmount transformer | 30–70 |
| Unit substation transformer | 45–80 |
| Power transformer (> 10 MVA) | 90–156 |
| LV switchboard | 14–30 |
| LV draw-out switchgear | 24–45 |
| MV metal-clad switchgear | 36–70 |
| E-house | 40–78 |

## Where the clock really starts

Most quoted lead times start at **drawing approval**, not at the purchase order. Between the two sit:

1. **Submittal preparation** — usually two to four weeks after the PO
2. **Review and approval** — the engineer and owner review, often with one round of comments and a resubmission

That alone can add six weeks or more. After the equipment ships, you still need delivery, installation and terminations, inspection and utility connection, and testing and commissioning. A transformer with a "40-week lead time" is often a **55-week decision** from order to energization.

## What drives long lead times

- **Demand.** Large new loads, utility upgrades and renewable projects are all competing for the same factory capacity.
- **Materials.** Grain-oriented electrical steel and copper drive much of a transformer's cost and schedule.
- **Components.** Vacuum breakers, power circuit breakers and protective relays come from a small number of sources, so switchgear dates follow theirs.
- **Customization.** Non-standard voltages, impedances, enclosures and accessories all add engineering time.
- **Test slots.** Large transformers need factory test time that books up months ahead.

## Seven ways to keep equipment off the critical path

1. **Release the long-lead items first.** Order transformers and MV switchgear from the approved single-line diagram, before the rest of the electrical package is final.
2. **Approve drawings quickly.** Agree a review turnaround with the engineer and owner, and plan for one resubmission.
3. **Specify standard ratings.** A standard voltage, impedance and kVA can cut months. Ask what is in stock before you specify a one-off.
4. **Ask for stock or surplus.** For urgent or replacement needs, [in-stock equipment](/inventory) can ship in days.
5. **Make bids comparable.** Ask every bidder to state lead time from approved drawings, test inclusions and exceptions. Our [RFQ and bid scorecard](/resources/rfq-scorecard) does this.
6. **Consider Canadian-made.** Domestic production shortens shipping, avoids border delays and makes factory visits and witnessed tests easy.
7. **Plan backwards.** Work from the energization date to an order-by date for every item with the [long-lead equipment planner](/resources/lead-time-planner).

## A worked example

A plant needs power on by **1 October**. Its 2,500 kVA padmount has a quoted lead time of 50 weeks from approved drawings.

- Commissioning: 2 weeks
- Inspection and utility connection: 3 weeks
- Installation and terminations: 4 weeks
- Delivery: 2 weeks
- Manufacturing: 50 weeks
- Review and approval: 3 weeks
- Submittal preparation: 3 weeks

Total: **67 weeks**. The purchase order has to be placed roughly 15 to 16 months before energization — long before many projects have even finished design.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to get a transformer in Canada?

Small dry-type transformers in standard ratings can be available in weeks, or from stock. Three-phase padmounts commonly take 30 to 70 weeks from approved drawings, and large power transformers can take two to three years.

### Why are transformer lead times so long?

Demand from data centres, electrification and grid upgrades has outpaced factory capacity, and key materials and components such as electrical steel, copper and breakers are constrained.

### When does a lead time start?

Usually when the manufacturer receives approved drawings, not when you place the purchase order. Add submittal and review time before it.

### How can I get a transformer faster?

Choose a standard rating, ask for in-stock or surplus units, approve drawings quickly and compare suppliers on lead time from approved drawings. For emergencies, ask about expedited builds.
